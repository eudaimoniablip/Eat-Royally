**Chapter 17: Eighty-Six**
---
The flashing green button on the executive console illuminated the cold sweat on Jin Woo’s forehead. The vault was suffocatingly silent, save for the rhythmic, recycled hum of the closed-loop HVAC system. Above him sat seventy stories of structural steel, fiber-optic networks, and a century of hoarded, blood-stained capital. Below him stood a mythological auditor, holding a gun to the head of the working class and asking Jin what his legacy was actually worth.


Jin looked at the glowing security monitors. He saw Elias Thorne, a mercenary who had bled for him, standing in front of the shattered glass with a drawn combat knife. He saw the terrified SWAT officers scrambling to drag the wounded out of the rain, their weapons completely useless against the architecture of the divine.


Jin swallowed the dry, copper taste in his mouth. He thought about his grandfather, Arthur, who had built this empire by chewing up mentors and spitting out their pensions. He thought about the fifty empty, lobotomized husks weeping in the subterranean bunker.


If I lock the doors, Jin realized, his cold, sociopathic logic finally fracturing under the impossible weight of the moment, I am just a vault for her to crack. She wants me to fight.


He looked up at Bunnie. The massive Opiliones hung from the ceiling tiles, its false yellow eyes staring at him through the dark. Jin exhaled a long, shuddering breath. The sociopath died, and the man remained.


He reached out and slammed his palm onto the green button.


He didn't just unlock the blast doors. He initiated the Zero Protocol.


Across the globe, cryptographic keys violently turned and permanently locked. Cayman Island shell companies evaporated into the digital ether. Billions in liquid assets violently decentralized, bleeding rapidly into public sectors, municipal bonds, and the restored pensions of the exact people Arthur Woo had structurally crushed. The Woo Telecom proprietary servers initiated a localized cascade failure, the server racks audibly screaming as the hard drives wiped a century of intellectual property completely clean.


Up on the ceiling, Bunnie tilted its grotesque head. For the first time in eight months, the massive parasite didn't click with ravenous hunger. It clicked with a strange, symbiotic stillness. The host had just excised his own ego. The food source was gone, but the terrarium was finally clean.


The heavy titanium blast shutters in the penthouse ground upward, exposing the stormy Seoul skyline. The magnetic locks on the elevators disengaged with a heavy, resonating _thunk_.


Jin Woo stood up. He unbuttoned his bespoke Italian jacket, slipped it off his shoulders, and dropped it onto the floor. He walked to the elevator with absolutely empty pockets.


Down in the lobby, the tension was a physical, crushing weight. Elias gripped his knife, the experimental carbon-fiber exoskeleton of his armor humming quietly as it strained against the ambient pressure. Rolo stood perfectly at his side, teeth bared, holding the line. Through the shattered glass doors, Shin stepped into the atrium.


The cleaner’s gravity well expanded violently around him. The imported Italian marble floor didn't just crack; it liquefied into fine dust beneath his leather shoes. The massive, decorative steel pillars of the lobby began to visibly bow and warp inward. Shin was preparing to compress the entire base of the skyscraper into a singularity.


The digital chime of the executive elevator cut through the silence like a gunshot. The polished steel doors slid open.


Jin stepped out into the atrium. He looked small. Stripped of his bodyguards, his billions, and his arrogant posture, he looked exactly like what he was: a twenty-six-year-old kid standing in the ruins of a shattered building.

Yeongon turned her massive, scaled head. The celestial heat radiating off her armored plates flash-boiled the damp night air blowing through the broken windows, filling the lobby with a hissing, metallic fog. She took a slow, heavy step toward him, the concrete foundation cracking like brittle ice beneath her claws.


Elias tensed, his muscles coiling to throw himself between them, but Jin raised a single hand, stopping the operative. Jin walked directly up to the apex predator. He didn't flinch as the blistering heat instantly singed his skin and burned the moisture from his eyes.


"The servers are wiped. The accounts are drained. The company is gone," Jin said, his voice remarkably, terrifyingly steady. He looked up into the lightless, void-black eyes of the beast. "I have nothing. I am nothing. The doors are open. Let them go."


Yeongon stared down at him. The silence stretched, heavy and vibrating with a 12-Hertz acoustic tension that made the remaining glass in the elevator shaft violently shatter.
Slowly, the unhinged, terrifying lines of her jaw shifted. The needle-like teeth retracted slightly into her gums.


She leaned down, her massive snout inches from Jin’s face. The smell of ozone, old earth, and boiling blood washed over him. She inhaled deeply, her nostrils flaring, actively searching the young man's soul for the heavy, arrogant, caloric scent of the Yangban.


She found nothing. The ego was entirely gone.


"You passed," Yeongon whispered. The metallic resonance of her voice carried a strange, ancient melancholy that vibrated in Jin's chest. "The ledger is locked."


She pulled back, raising her massive head toward the vaulted ceiling of the atrium.


Without the 100th soul, the bureaucratic ascension was mathematically impossible. The celestial mechanics demanded absolute perfection, but the earth had just provided an unresolvable paradox.
Deep inside her chest, the ninety-nine corrupt souls she had consumed realized they were permanently trapped. The mass violently, catastrophically expanded.


CRACK.


The final, lingering remnants of Yeongon's human silhouette shattered entirely. The heavy tungsten clasps of her corset—the last vestiges of her containment—exploded outward like military shrapnel.


Elias grabbed the handle of Rolo's harness and violently tackled Jin to the floor just as the shockwave hit, shielding the young man with his carbon-fiber rig.


Yeongon didn't just grow; she unfolded. Massive, serpentine coils of iridescent black scales erupted into the lobby, crushing the reception desks into powder. Thick, structural horns tore through her skull. She was an Imugi denied its pearl, instantly transforming into a creature of pure, unadulterated mass and wrath.


Shin raised his gloved hand. The spacetime around the cleaner literally screamed, visually tearing into jagged black fissures as he attempted to fold the colossal dragon into the void.
But Yeongon didn't look toward the heavens, and she didn't strike at Shin. She looked at the seventy stories of corporate steel above her. The rigged system.


With a deafening, multi-toned roar that ruptured the eardrums of every SWAT officer on the block, the dragon launched herself upward. She slammed her massive, scaled coils not into the void, but directly into the primary load-bearing pillars of the Woo Telecom Tower.


Thick, structural steel I-beams snapped like dry twigs. Millions of tons of concrete pulverized into blinding dust. She spiraled upward, tearing through the first floor, then the second, ripping the infrastructure to shreds from the inside out. She wasn't just destroying the building; she was weaponizing millions of tons of analog architecture against a god of gravity.


"Move!" Elias roared, hauling Jin to and Rolo to moving speed fueled by adrenaline. They scrambled out of the collapsing lobby, diving through the shattered glass doors and rolling violently onto the rain-slicked pavement.
Behind them, Shin looked up. The flawless composure on the cleaner's face finally fractured just as the spatial integrity of his earthworks was utterly, catastrophically overwhelmed by the sheer, analog weight of a collapsing skyscraper.


The seventy-story tower imploded. A mountain of twisted steel, shattered glass, and pulverized concrete dropped directly onto the Celestial Court's top cleaner, burying him alive beneath the ultimate symbol of human greed.
Out on the street, the SWAT officers watched in paralyzed horror. A massive, serpentine shadow coiled rapidly through the dust cloud of the collapsing steel, crushing the architecture of the 1% into a flattened mountain of twisted rubble before vanishing entirely into the dark, stormy sky.


Elias coughed violently, waving the thick, choking concrete dust from his scarred face. He looked down at his ruined tactical suit, then he jerked his neck around looking for Rolo just as the ninety-nine-pound Doberman licked his face standing calmly on the wet pavement , watching the apocalyptic destruction with a lightly wagging stump of a tail.


Jin sat on the curb, his face covered in gray ash. He was looking at the collapsed ruins of everything his family had ever built.


"She didn't ascend," Jin muttered, his voice entirely hollow, watching the massive dust cloud settle over the Seoul skyline.


"No," Elias said. He sheathed his combat knife with a sharp click and reached down to give Rolo a heavy, well-earned ear scratch. He pulled a crushed, synthetic cinnabar seal out of his pocket and tossed it casually into the gutter. "She didn't want to join their club, Jin. She just wanted to tear down the building."
