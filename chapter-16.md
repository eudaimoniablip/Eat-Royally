**Chapter 16: Prix Fixe **
---
The Woo Telecom Tower was a seventy-story monument to corporate hubris, wrapped in reinforced glass and steel. At 11:00 PM, it went entirely dark. High in the penthouse vault, Jin Woo initiated the Blackout Protocol. Heavy, interlocking titanium blast shutters slammed down over the floor-to-ceiling windows with the deafening, final clang of a bank vault sealing shut. The elevators are magnetically locked at the ground floor. The air filtration system shifted to a closed-loop internal supply, the room instantly tasting of recycled ozone and stale copper.


Jin sat alone, relatively alone, in the center of the dark executive suite, watching the lobby feed on a bank of battery-powered monitors. He had inherited an empire, and he was prepared to bury himself in it to survive.


"We’re not going down without a fight, right, Bunnie?" Jin whispered, his voice fracturing as he finally gave a name to his symbiotic roommate.


From the deep shadows of the vaulted ceiling, a massive shape silently detached itself. The Opiliones had grown exponentially since the Hawaiian estate. It was now the size of a full-grown African lion. Its glossy obsidian chitin absorbed the faint, bluish glow of the monitors as its eight impossibly long, thick, red-banded legs navigated down the structural pillars with frictionless grace.


It settled directly onto the polished mahogany desk, looming over Jin. Its central body—a grotesque, terrifying fusion of a severed wolf's snout and sharp, rabbit-like ears—tilted drastically to the side in a posture resembling a curious, endearing puppy. Its false yellow eyes glowed brightly in the dark. It didn't offer protection; it offered a service. As Jin's carefully maintained 65-BPM heart rate finally spiked into genuine, suffocating terror, Bunnie let out a vibrating, clicking purr, happily gorging itself on the massive wave of cortisol flooding the airtight room.


Down in the lobby, Elias Thorne slammed his fist against the reinforced steel of the elevator bank. "Jin, you cowardly son of a bitch, open the doors!" Elias barked into his comms.


Static hissed back. Jin had severed the connection. The sociopath had done exactly what sociopaths do—he built a wall out of everyone else.


Elias was trapped in the sprawling glass atrium. Rolo paced at his side, the ninety-nine-pound Doberman letting out a low, continuous growl, his hackles raised so high he looked like a wolf ready to snap.


Outside the glass doors, the flashing red and blue lights of the Seoul Metropolitan Police illuminated the rain-slicked streets. Three dozen heavily armed SWAT officers had surrounded the building, responding to the silent alarms Jin had tripped to create a buffer of meat shields.


Then, the streetlights at the end of the block shattered. It wasn't an explosion; the bulbs simply burst, the glass imploding as the localized atmospheric pressure violently dropped.


Yeongon walked into the intersection. The human suit was completely gone. She was a towering, biomechanical nightmare of celestial architecture. Thick, iridescent copperish-obsidian scales armored her from her throat to her claws. Heat radiated off her in visible, warping waves, evaporating the torrential rain into hissing steam before it could even touch her skin. Her jaw hung slightly unhinged, revealing rows of translucent, needle-like teeth, and her eyes were twin voids of absolute, lightless gravity.


"Target acquired! Open fire!" the SWAT commander screamed, absolute terror fracturing his voice as the officers stared at the proto-dragon stomping its way down the road.


Thirty assault rifles opened up on the street. It was a deafening, pointless cacophony. The 5.56mm rounds struck Yeongon's scaled mass and simply flattened like raindrops hitting an anvil, sparking harmlessly off her celestial armor and dropping into the puddles.


Suddenly, the gunfire abruptly ceased. It wasn't a ceasefire; it was an exterior shutdown. A perfectly tailored charcoal suit materialized in the center of the intersection. Shin. He didn't look at the police or the sparking bullet casings. He just clicked his silver watch ope, his eyes scanning the environment for his target.


Instantly, the rain stopped falling, suspended mid-air. All thirty SWAT officers and their four-ton armored vans were pinned flat to the wet asphalt by an invisible, crushing gravitational weight. The suspension systems of the vehicles blew out simultaneously. The entire block was locked in absolute, hydrostatic stasis.


Shin looked up at Yeongon. "The extraction is canceled. I am collapsing the grid."


Yeongon didn't even break her stride. She ignored Shin entirely, bulldozing right through the edge of his gravitational anomaly, and smashed through the reinforced glass of the Woo Telecom lobby.


Elias drew his combat knife, planting his boots firmly onto the marble. Rolo snarled, stepping in front of Elias, ready to hold the line against a god.


But Yeongon didn't attack. She stopped in the center of the atrium, the heat radiating off her scales warping the air around Elias's face. She looked up at the corner of the ceiling, staring directly into the blinking red lens of the primary security camera.


When she spoke, her voice didn't echo in the lobby. She bypassed the acoustics entirely, projecting the frequency directly through the CCTV feed and into the dark penthouse vault where Jin sat shivering.


"Look at them, Jin," Yeongon’s voice vibrated through the speakers in the executive suite. It was the heavy, metallic resonance of a collapsing star, rattling the coffee cup on his desk. "Look at the mercenary you hired. Look at the police officers bleeding on the pavement outside. The working class, bleeding to protect the Yangban in his castle."


Up in the vault, Jin stared at the monitors. His breath hitched. Beside him, Bunnie tilted its head the other way, clicking rapidly as the room filled with the scent of impending death.


"I am at ninety-nine," Yeongon continued, her lightless eyes staring through the camera lens. "You are the last Yangban. You will feed me my ticket to raze the unjust heavens above!"


She raised a single, scaled hand, pointing a sword-length clawed finger at Elias and the terrified cops outside the shattered doors.


"I offer you a choice of the ending," she declared, the sheer acoustic weight of her words forcing Elias to grit his teeth. "Keep the blast doors locked. Protect your stock portfolios, your legacy, and your grandfather's empire. If you do, I will tear this lobby apart. I will consume the souls of these men, and when I am done, I will rip the titanium off your vault and eat your ego as my hundredth meal. You will die a parasite."


She lowered her hand. The silence stretched, thick and suffocating.


"Or," she whispered, the sound chilling Jin to the bone through the speakers, "you can prove you are a true nobleman. Liquidate the trusts. Wipe the servers and transfer the Woo empire to the public sector. Open your blast doors, and walk down here with empty pockets to save their lives. If you do... there will be nothing left in you for me to eat."


She bluffed. She knew the mind of the Yangban. He would sooner burn the world to the ground than admit defeat and abandon his dragon’s hoard.


Up in the vault, Jin Woo looked at the flashing green button on his console that controlled the blast doors and the corporate server wipes. He had spent his entire life building walls out of money.


Now, he had sixty seconds to decide if the money was a fortress, or a tomb.
